In evaluating the debate over whether "Inception" is the best movie of all time, it is evident that both sides presented compelling arguments; however, upon closer inspection, one side emerges as more convincing. 

The affirmative side argues for "Inception" on the basis of its intricate narrative structure, innovative blending of genres, and the depth of its thematic exploration. It emphasizes that the film invites intense viewer engagement and rewards those willing to delve into its complexities. This promotes critical thinking, setting it apart from more conventional offerings. Furthermore, it presents Cobb's emotional journey as a multifaceted experience that resonates with viewers, thereby establishing "Inception" as a revolutionary cinema experience.

Conversely, the opposing side effectively critiques "Inception's" complexity, arguing that this very intricacy detracts from its emotional impact and accessibility to a wider audience. It points out that other films, such as "The Shawshank Redemption" and "Schindler's List," achieve substantial emotional resonance while simultaneously engaging with profound themes, all without alienating viewers with convoluted narratives. The argument extends to highlight that true cinematic greatness encompasses both emotional depth and accessibility.

Throughout the rounds, the rebuttals on both sides were robust. The affirmative side defended its position by emphasizing the immersive nature of "Inception," asserting that the active engagement required from the audience enhances its experience. This was met with a counterpoint highlighting the risks of confusion among viewers craving more straightforward storytelling. 

However, the insistence on complexity as a form of engagement falters against the opposing arguments. While "Inception" indeed pushes boundaries in narrative innovation, this strength comes with a significant cost to its accessibility and emotional reach. The critiques effectively illustrate that a film’s ability to connect with its audience on a fundamental level—a key aspect of cinematic success—may be overshadowed by a narrative puzzle that is more intellectually stimulating than universally relatable.

In conclusion, the opposing side's emphasis on emotional depth coupled with broader accessibility presents a stronger case for defining cinematic greatness. Thus, while "Inception" is a remarkable film with many commendable qualities, the arguments presented make a compelling case that it cannot be categorized as the best movie of all time when assessed through the lens of universal appeal and emotional resonance. Therefore, the debate tilts in favor of the opposing side.
